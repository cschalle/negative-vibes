Deconvolution: three ways to spend one measurement

OpticFilm 8200i at 3600 dpi · PSF measured 2026-11-01 from a razor blade · crops from the same dusty frame, green-weighted, inverted with one shared stretch so only the filter differs

The scanner's point spread function is now measured on both axes, so its transfer function can be inverted. What the measurement does not decide is how hard to invert it. A linear filter multiplies signal and noise by the same amount at every frequency, so deconvolution cannot improve signal-to-noise — it corrects rendition, and it makes grain louder in exactly the proportion it makes detail louder. The choice below is how much of that trade to take.

Below about 40 cyc/mm the frame carries several times more picture than noise, so restoring that band restores mostly picture. By 50 it is a coin toss and by 60 it is mostly grain. Every difference you can see further down comes from what each filter does on either side of that crossover.

What each one is doing

Constant λ 0.08 — restore everywhere, equally

One regularisation constant across the whole band. It pulls the MTF closest to flat and gives the highest MTF50, 44.2 against 29.6 as scanned. It also amplifies 50–70 cyc/mm by 1.9×, and up there the frame is 1.1× to 0.2× over its noise floor, so most of what gets amplified is grain. In the flat scene below this is the one that looks like it has been through a sharpening filter.

True Wiener — the MSE-optimal answer

Regularise by the measured signal-to-noise at each frequency, which is the textbook Wiener filter and the one that minimises mean squared error. It judges everything above 40 cyc/mm to be mostly noise and suppresses it, ending up smoother than the unfiltered scan and barely sharper: MTF50 30.2 against 29.6. Mathematically correct, and it gives up almost all the value of having measured the PSF.

Hybrid — restore to the crossover, then stop

Constant λ 0.08 up to 40 cyc/mm, then blend into the SNR-weighted rolloff over the next 20. Identical to the constant filter everywhere the film has real content to recover, and identical to Wiener where it does not. MTF50 40.8 — 92% of the constant filter's gain — for a noise amplification of 1.19× instead of 1.57×.

All three are points on one curve: where you put the crossover between "restore" and "suppress". That is a single number, which is what a strength control in the UI would expose. The three buttons above are that control at three settings.

Caveats worth knowing before choosing

These crops are a plain inversion with one shared stretch, not the full pipeline, so colour and tone are not what the application would show. Sharpness and grain are what they will be, because deconvolution runs on the linear raw before conversion.

Filtering pushes 4.7% of samples below zero, the most negative being −0.035. That is ringing around edges and it gets clipped. In a negative the near-zero end is the densest part of the film, which is the highlights of the positive, so clipping there is not free and is not yet measured.

One kernel is used for the whole frame. Within a single frame each carrier's own tilt moved the measured rise from 2.57 to 3.61 px one way and 2.64 to 4.03 the other — a factor of 1.5, larger than the difference between the two axes. Field dependence is the next thing to measure, and it may matter more than this choice.
